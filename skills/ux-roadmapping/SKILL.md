# UX Roadmapping

Turn a team's inputs into a strategic, living UX roadmap: outcomes and problems to solve, with evidence, priorities, and an explicit plan for learning. Use the bundled method to connect goals, evidence and tradeoffs to outcomes.

Read [the method and attribution](references/course-method.md) before applying the method. For a workshop, also read [workshop formats](references/workshops.md). Adapt [the roadmap pack](assets/roadmap-pack.md) to the requested deliverable; omit fields that add no decision value.

## Start with the team's actual context

Reuse information already supplied. Ask only for missing information that materially changes the roadmap, preferably in one short set of questions:
- Which team/product and scope: cross-functional product, UX field, or research/design/content specialty?
- What user outcomes and business goals matter, and for which audience and planning horizon?
- What inputs exist: research, analytics, strategy, current commitments, incoming asks, dependencies, and available capacity?
- Who owns the roadmap, who contributes, who consumes it, and who resolves competing priorities?

If the user wants an immediate draft, work from the available inputs. Label assumptions, unknown metrics, proposed owners and unconfirmed scope. Do not invent evidence, capacity, deadlines, decision rights or stakeholder agreement. Historical notes are context, not proof of current team conditions.

Default to a reviewable Markdown draft. Use the requested tool or format when specified; do not create a new tracking system merely to make a roadmap.

## Build or refresh

1. **Frame the roadmap.** State title, team/scope, creator, date, audience, high-level goals, horizon definitions and exclusions. A UX roadmap should connect to the product strategy and partner work. When influence or scope is limited, offer a specialty pilot instead of pretending to own the cross-functional roadmap.
2. **Collect evidence and requests.** Give each source an ID. Separate observations, interpretations, assumptions, requested solutions and actual commitments. Preserve research dates, segments, limitations and conflicting evidence. Use a journey knowledge map when evidence gaps are unclear.
3. **Create themes.** Describe a beneficiary's need or desired outcome, linked to a business objective. Group supporting input IDs, retain unmatched inputs, and distinguish themes from possible solutions or tickets. Name themes at comparable levels before ranking them. For solo AI theming, apply the disagreement and quote-verification safeguards in the reference.
4. **Prioritize transparently.** Agree on criteria before presenting rankings. Explain user value, goal contribution, evidence confidence, effort/capacity, dependencies and any real constraints. Treat needs confidence and solution confidence separately when they differ. Missing numbers are unknowns, not fabricated scores. A low-confidence important need may justify discovery in Now without committing to a solution. Show what is displaced when accepting a new request.
5. **Visualize and share.** Place themes in Now, Next, Future with decreasing specificity. Define these horizons for this team, because horizon definitions vary across organizations. Now is sufficiently scoped to act on; Next needs additional sequencing/validation; Future stays broad and changeable. Distinguish proposed work from confirmed commitments. Put delivery tickets or sprint schedules in a linked execution view, not in place of the strategic roadmap.
6. **Revisit.** Propose a named owner, review cadence and triggers such as new evidence, changed goals, capacity or dependencies. Record movement between horizons, why it happened and what was displaced. Say whether the draft is proposed, reviewed or approved based on actual evidence.

For a refresh, explain changes from the previous version and retain a decision trail. Avoid regenerating a new roadmap that silently drops prior commitments.

## Deliver something the team can use

Scale to the user's request. An ordinary team-roadmap draft should include:
- A short strategic frame and explicit horizon definitions.
- A visual Now/Next/Future chart with outcome themes, plus an accessible text equivalent and supporting register.
- A theme register connecting each theme to beneficiary, need, business objective, evidence, confidence, success signal, proposed/confirmed owner, dependencies and next learning/action step.
- A resource plan with role-specific effort ranges, allocations, elapsed duration and capacity gaps.
- A priority rationale, displaced/deferred work and significant assumptions or gaps.
- The next decision needed, review date/cadence proposal and source links.

Do not force every field into one unreadably wide table. Keep the board compact and put detail in theme cards or a supporting register. Show metric baseline and target only when provided or clearly marked as proposals. Without capacity, provide provisional sequencing rather than a promise that everything fits.

For a stakeholder's prescribed solution, use the Confidence Reframe: listen to the request, translate it to a user outcome, check evidence for the need and solution, and present the tradeoff. Do not manufacture displaced work or claim authority to accept the change.

## Resourcing and effort

Read [resourcing and effort planning](references/resourcing.md) when creating or revising a roadmap. Include a resource plan by default: proposed/confirmed lead and needed roles, effort range by role in person-days, available allocation, estimated elapsed duration, dependencies and confidence. Show a short staffing/effort line on each visual theme and a supporting capacity view. If inputs are missing, give clearly labeled assumptions/scenarios and identify what needs confirmation.

Keep effort, elapsed time, headcount and FTE distinct. Account for existing work, leave, BAU, skill constraints, shared specialists and overlapping assignments. Do not assume adding designers reduces time proportionally. Show overloaded periods or uncertain capacity and give explicit scope, sequence, date or staffing tradeoffs. Discovery, validation and delivery support need resources too; a design estimate does not establish engineering delivery time.

## Visual roadmap output

Include a visual chart with the supporting detail unless the user requests text only. Default to outcome themes arranged in Now / Next / Future columns. Show research, design and validation work under each theme where relevant, using consistent labels and a small legend. Keep the chart readable at presentation size; place evidence and ownership detail in a companion register or expandable details.

Use an inline visual when the environment supports one; otherwise provide a Mermaid flowchart or a standalone HTML/SVG chart the user can view. Do not require a proprietary design tool or a specific visualization skill. Preserve a text equivalent for accessibility. Use a dated timeline or swimlanes only when real dates, dependencies and workstreams are known. Distinguish targets, assumptions and commitments, and do not imply duration or parallel capacity through invented bar lengths. Label fictional examples prominently. Honor a user-requested output format.

## Review before delivery

Check that each theme changes something for a beneficiary and connects to a business goal; evidence IDs resolve; direct quotes exactly match the source; uncertainties remain visible; priorities explain real tradeoffs; Now fits known capacity or is marked provisional; named-person overlaps and role-specific capacity are checked; and the roadmap has an owner and a way to change. Do not label a draft committed or imply the team approved it.

## Source use and privacy

This independent skill is informed by UX roadmapping practice and personal learning from NN/g's UX Roadmaps course. It is not an official NN/g product or a substitute for the course. Read the method reference for attribution and limits. No course PDFs, slides, recordings, screenshots or raw personal notes are distributed here.

The bundled method is self-contained. Use the user's current research and goals; no Obsidian vault or Granola connection is required. Do not invent teachings from unavailable source materials or infer that a user attended a course. When saving in a repository or knowledge base, follow its local instructions and preserve raw sources. Creating a roadmap does not authorize sending it, changing team tickets or publishing private research. Write in plain language without em dashes.
